*Saturday 14 September, 23:47. The Restricted Archives, three floors below the library.*

## THE STACK

The Restricted Archives were three floors down a spiral staircase that had been built for people carrying books and not for people running, and the last flight had a landing on it that nobody cleaned, and Eli Park found that he could feel the shape of the drain in his shoe.

The wards down here were so strong that Lyra could barely get a reading on Milo four feet away. It felt like being put into a coat two sizes too large. She had not known that you could lose *people* and still be in the same room as them.

"Dimensional Incidents," said Milo. "Classified. That is the whole shelf. That is the entire shelf and there are eleven boxes and nine of them say nineteen ninety-seven on the spine and one of them says nineteen forty-one and I have not opened the nineteen forty-one one and I would like to record that I have not opened it."

"Open the nineteen forty-one one."

"No. I would like to *record*—"

"Open it after."

He opened the nine ninety-seven ones instead, and they put them on a reading table under a lamp that came on when Milo put his hand near it, and it was, for about four minutes, the calmest any of them had been since Wednesday.

Then Eli found the second set of blueprints underneath the incident report and everything after that happened at once and to everybody.

## THE FIRST THING, WHICH WAS A PHOTOGRAPH

It was a group photograph, taken before, and there were five people in it, and it was the ordinary kind of photograph that institutions keep: a group outside a building on a day that went wrong later.

Harlow was second from the left, and he was younger, and he had his hand round the shoulders of a Japanese woman of about thirty with her hair up.

Beside her, holding both their hands, was a girl of six or seven with her hair in two plaits and an expression of open hostility towards the camera.

"She's got his jaw," said Zara. "That's the daughter. She's the daughter, isn't she."

"Missing, not dead." Eli had the incident report. He had it before Lyra did, because he always got there first and then waited, and she had worked out over three days that this was not coldness. "Two confirmed dead, three missing presumed dead. And then the missing list, and the missing list says *Aiko Harlow, thirty-four* and *Mei Harlow, seven*."

"Thirty-four is not thirty-two," said Milo.

"Thirty-four is what the file says."

"Thirty-four is two years out in the same direction as everything else in this room," said Milo, and he was not being clever, he was being aggrieved, and Lyra understood the aggrievance, which was that in the last three days a boy in this building had been on a machine and a girl had signed a card for herself and the file had said *no survivors detected* nine hours before the boy was found alive.

Zara had both hands flat on the table. Her eyes were shut.

"Zara."

"Sorry. Sorry. Give me a second. It's not — I'm not doing the thing." She opened her eyes. "There's residue on all of it. Not information. It feels like a room after an argument."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the only honest thing I've got and I'm not going to pretend it's a reading." She took her hands off the table. "Lyra, they've been *erased*. Both of them. That's not sad, that's — that's the specific texture of a person who's had something taken out. I've felt it on people whose dead have been stolen out of them and it doesn't feel like grief, it feels like a *hole with a shape*. If that's what's on this file then he isn't a monster, he's a man who's had his family taken and not told."

"He has a machine that harvests students."

"Yes. Both. Can they not both be true, and can we stop *arguing about which one is more real*, because we have about ten minutes before somebody comes down those stairs?"

They looked at each other across a reading table in a basement.

"I want to burn it," Lyra said.

"Say that again."

"The whole box. I want to put it in a corridor and burn it, and I have wanted to do that since about half past eleven, and I am telling you because I think if I don't say it out loud I'll do it and then I will not have told anybody." She was aware that her voice was doing something she had not authorised. "Because every single person who has ever gone near this has got it in their head for the rest of their life. You have got it now. You have got *Kyoto* in your head and a photograph of a child and you are sixteen, and he did that on purpose, and that is the *point* of it. He put it in a box and he put the box where a sixteen-year-old would find it, and that is not an accident, that is a *method*."

Nobody said anything.

"That's the most frightening thing anybody has said to me this week," said Eli.

"Then I will say it again, because it is true and because I am not going to pretend I did not say it." Lyra put her hands flat on the table as well. "But I am not burning it. Because if I burn it then the next person finds nothing, and the next person is whoever he picks next, and there is no box that stops him, there is only this one."

"Then what," said Milo.

"Copy everything. Photograph all of it. Leave the box exactly as it was so that nobody can say we took it, and go to Weaver tonight, before the shift change at midnight, and if she will not do anything then we go to the Council in the morning and we go *public*."

"That is a good plan," said Eli.

"It is a *procedural* plan," said Milo. "It has got a box in it and a signature in it and it assumes that a woman we have never met, at eleven forty at night, will act on the word of four first-years. It is also the only plan any of us has said out loud, and I hate it, and I am not going to be the one who says no. Say no, Lyra. Say it and give me a reason."

"I don't have one."

"Then we do yours." He took out his phone. "Both of us can be wrong, and we can both be doing it, and the *four* of us can be wrong in four different directions, and then nobody can shut it down with one phone call." He looked up. "That's four numbers and four addresses and four people who do not know each other's names. I'm not being brave. I'm being annoying. It's a different skill and it is the one I have."

Eli put his phone face down on the table with both hands on it, deliberately, and Lyra understood that he had been out-argued by somebody using his own tactic and that he was going to be fine about it later and unbearable about it now.

"Do it," she said. "All four of you. And Eli—"

"Yes."

"The photographs establish that she was there. That's all they establish. Remember that, because in about a month I am going to want them to establish more than that, and they won't."

## THE SECOND THING, WHICH WAS A DRAWING

The Conduit schematics were under the incident report, folded twice, and the paper was forty years old and had been unfolded and refolded so many times that it went soft.

Eli spread them flat and put his hands on the table either side and did not touch them for eleven seconds, which was the longest any of them had ever seen him not touch something.

"There are two sets of handwriting on this," he said. "Look. The heavy one is his, and it's the same hand as the signatory line on the letter of authorisation. And this—" he put a finger down beside the margin, without touching it "—this is small and fast and it argues with him. In the margin. Nine times."

The letter of authorisation was dated the eleventh of June, two thousand and nine, and it was signed by a Council officer, and the name at the bottom of it was Yuen.

"That is a person," said Lyra.

"That is a *person*, and she is on the Council, and she signed the thing that lets him do this, and I want to know whether she read it." Eli read down the margin in the small fast hand, and his voice went strange. "'*This assumes the reference tone is a person. It is not. No living subject has been located. Do not proceed on the assumption that one will be.*' And then, in his hand: '*She will be.*'"

Nobody spoke.

"And again, in hers: '*Whose daughter?*'"

It sat on the table.

"There's a page missing," said Eli. "Between this and the next sheet, there is a page missing, because the numbering jumps from forty-one to forty-three and the binding has been cut and re-cut and I have looked at the cut with my own eyes."

"Then somebody took it out."

"Somebody took it out, and then somebody put the whole thing back in a classified box where a sixteen-year-old would find it on the fourth worst week of her life." Eli sat down on the edge of the table. "Lyra. There's a scheduling line at the bottom of forty-three. It says *subject selection — on the basis of a lineage match, when a candidate of sufficient tone is identified*."

"Is there a name?"

"There's a reference." He turned the sheet so she could see it. "*See file: L.H.C. — Animus Argenti index, 2002.*"

L.H.C.

L.H.C.

"Lin Ho Chen," said Zara.

Nobody said it for a moment, and then Lyra said it, and it was, she understood afterwards, the moment the evening turned over.

"She's been in the file the whole time. She's not a warning in the margin. She's the *index*."

## THE THIRD THING, WHICH WAS THE PHOTOGRAPH THEY SHOULD HAVE READ FIRST

At the back of the box, under the last incident sheet, there was a print that had been folded small and folded often and unfolded so many times that the folds had gone through.

It was a photograph of the garden at the Zen school in Kyoto on the day of the collapse. It was a bad photograph, taken from a low wall, probably by somebody's brother, and it showed a hole in the world about nine feet across with a woman on her knees beside it.

Lyra had to put the lamp on her hand to keep her hands still.

"That's her."

"Lyra—"

"That's *her*. That's the way she's standing. That's—" She stopped. "That's how she stood at the top of the stairs in the studio in Ellery Street, doing the thing with the cup, with her weight on her back leg. I have been copying that stance since I was five years old and I did not know I was copying it."

Zara had gone white. "Lyra, there's somebody at the *edge* of it. On the left. That's not part of the collapse, that's a person standing next to it on purpose."

Eli took the lamp.

He looked at it for some time. Then he said, in a voice that had gone carefully flat:

"There's no name on this photograph. There's no caption. It was not in the incident file — it was in the *box*, behind everything, folded small. And Lyra — your mother is not on the roster of the Kyoto research team. There is no roster entry. I have been through the roster twice."

"So she wasn't there."

"She was there. That is *exactly* what I am saying. A photograph establishes that she was in that garden on that afternoon and not one other thing. It does not establish that she was running the experiment. It does not establish that she was a warning or a conspirator or a victim. It establishes that she was *there*, and there is a document somewhere in this building that says a woman was not, and one of those two is a lie and we cannot tell which from a photograph in a basement."

"Eli—"

"And that," he said, "is the first time in my life anybody has needed me to say a thing I did not want to say, and I am going to want it noted that I noticed I was doing it."

## THE ALARM

It was Zara.

She put her hand flat on the wall and said, very quietly, "Something in the wall just woke up."

There was a sound like a match being struck very far off, and then a sound like a match being struck, and then the reading lamp went out.

In the dark, the black-silver thread came down out of the stonework of the ceiling — not fast, not dramatic, *thoroughly* — and it went along the shelf, and it went along the reading table, and it stopped at the box, and it began, patiently, to record.

"How long," said Eli.

"It was asleep at eleven o'clock when we came down," said Zara. "It has been asleep every time we have been in this room and it is not asleep now and I would like everybody to notice that nobody has told us what wakes it up."

Nobody had told them.

"It is us," said Milo. "It is obviously us. It is a floor sensor and we are four warm bodies and one of us is a—"

"I know what I am."

"Then stop, because I am not doing it, and Eli is photographing, and Lyra is standing there, and you can feel the wall. That is enough. That is more than enough." Milo's voice cracked in the middle of it. "I am not — Eli. Tell her to stop feeling the wall."
