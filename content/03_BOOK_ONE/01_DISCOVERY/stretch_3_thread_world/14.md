# Chapter 14: The Thing Under the Floor

## NINETY SECONDS AHEAD

Dr Mei-Hua Chen was sixty-eight seconds ahead of them for eleven days, and the reason was not intelligence and was not training and was the single most useful thing Lyra Chen learned in the autumn of 2013.

She did not go to Security. She walked, at a perfectly ordinary pace, along a corridor, past a fire door, and through a fire door, and down a service stair, and the service stair went to a sub-level that Lyra Chen had walked past nine days ago on her first afternoon and had thought was a *store*, and the Registrar unlocked it with a key on a chain round her neck and did not say anything, and let the door stand open, which was the message.

Then she said, "Four of you. Two of you are about to do something brave and I would like to prevent it, so I am going to tell you what is going to happen in the next four hours and then you are going to help me, and at some point after that you are going to be extremely angry with me, and I am going to deserve it, and I have decided to deserve it in advance so that neither of us has to waste any of the next four hours on it."

"Go on," said Eli Park.

"There is a machine under this building." Dr. Chen opened a door. "I have known there was a machine under this building for eleven years. I have never seen it. It is not in the Council's inventory. It is not in *my* inventory, and I have been the Registrar of this Academy for eleven years, and I have signed for the removal of a boiler from that sub-level in 2011 and the man who came to do the work would not go past the second door and would not say why, and I did not ask why, and I have thought about that every day for two years."

*Lyra looked at her. This is the aunt she had spoken to for eleven minutes in a corridor twelve days ago, who had said, you are the first Chen in this family in four generations who was not the smartest person in the room, and it is a relief, and I am sorry, and I am also extremely glad.*

"There is a girl on the third floor of the east dormitory," said Dr. Chen, "who went into that room on the eleventh of September at twenty-three fourteen hours, in exchange for a card that she signed herself, which she was fifteen, and I discovered this morning in a corridor because a boy of sixteen handed me a piece of paper, and I have had this morning to decide what I am going to do about it, and I have decided that I am going to do everything the Council requires and *not one thing more*, and I would like the four of you to help me with the part that the Council cannot do."

"Which is what?"

"Find out whether there is anybody else down there," said Dr. Mei-Hua Chen. "That is all. I am not asking you to rescue her. I have four wardens and a warrant and a Headmistress and I am going to do all of that properly and slowly and in writing, and I am going to be slow, because the last four times somebody in this building went fast in a sub-level a boy of fourteen is now on a machine somewhere and a woman of twenty-four has been in a cavity under Kyoto for sixteen years."

## THE BOY WITH THE SHEET

The assessment suite was in the East Tower's north annexe and it was on a Thursday-to-Sunday rota, and it was staffed, on the Friday morning, by a second-year and a technician, and the Registrar of Threadweaver Academy walked in at eleven o'clock and asked them to step outside, and they did, and one of them cried, and the other one did not, and that is in the record because the other one was asked about it.

James Chen was in a chair with headphones on and a sheet of paper on his knee and a sum half-finished in a school exercise book, and he was fourteen years old, and he had been in the building for forty hours.

"James," said Lyra.

"Lyra." He took the headphones off one ear. "I finished it. That's the thing I wanted to say. I've finished the thing on the sheet and it *goes*, Lyra, it goes to a place and then it comes back, and yesterday it was doing it on its own about every eleven seconds, and I have written down every single time, and there are one hundred and forty-one, and I have got a *graph*—"

He held up the exercise book, and it was a graph, and it was drawn in the back of a school exercise book by a fourteen-year-old, and it was the most frightening object Lyra Chen ever saw.

"Bring it to me," said Dr. Chen. "Both of you. Now, please. And leave everything on the desk exactly as it is, and I want the room locked, and I want the lock to be a chair under the door, because I have a better lock and I do not want a record of it."

---

They got him out at eleven twenty.

That was the entire margin. Everything that happened after that happened because a woman of sixty-eight years old had been sixty-eight seconds ahead of everybody for eleven days and had known, on the eleventh of September, what she was looking at, and had spent eleven days finding a way to get a boy out of a chair that did not require anybody in this building to be brave.

And here is the thing about it, which is in the transcript and which Lyra has asked to be allowed to be the first thing anybody says at a conference about the autumn of 2013:

Nobody had to be brave. That is the whole of the finding. The Inquiry established, over nine days, that the events of September 2013 in Threadweaver Academy were not a story about four teenagers. They are a story about how much *ordinary, competent, unheroic procedure* this building was capable of if somebody would only let it happen, and about a woman of sixty-eight who had known for eleven years that there was a machine under her and had spent eleven days quietly finding out which four of the eleven hundred people in this building could be trusted to do a filing job.

Eli Park's name is in it because he handed her a piece of paper in a corridor in daylight with a witness.

Zara Washington's is in it because she asked, in a lecture hall of two hundred and eleven people, whether anybody knew what the number measured, and because a professor of mathematics in a corridor said that a girl of sixteen was braver than a committee, in public, on the record.

Milo Reyes's is in it because he said the number was loud enough to hear.

Lyra Chen's is in it because a man in a demonstration hall had touched her arm on the twelfth of September and she had not washed it, and because on the thirteenth of September, at about a quarter past eleven in the morning, she asked Dr. Chen one question and then did not ask it again:

"Does he know?" she said. "James. Does he know what this is?"

"No," said Dr. Mei-Hua Chen.

"Will somebody tell him?"

"On Tuesday, in his own words, with his mother in the room, by a doctor and not by a member of faculty." She was already walking. "And not before, and I have spent eleven days on that, and the reason I have spent eleven days on it is that I read a transcript in June of a hearing in another country in which a child of eleven was told, by a man with a clipboard, and has spent the rest of her life being careful with the word *volunteer*."

## THE ARCHWAY

They went into the Thread Maze at 2:40 on the Friday afternoon with institutional permission, which is the sentence that appears in every account and which is the sentence that a reader of the 2013 transcript should stop at.

*Permission had been given by the Registrar of the Academy to four first-year students to enter a sealed assessment structure, on the grounds of a reading, in writing, on a piece of paper, which is a piece of paper. It was given over the written objection of the Consultant Engineer, who had said he would not sign it and would not be responsible for it, and it was given subject to three conditions: that the arch was entered and left by the span and not by the rubble, that the timber was not to be leaned on, and that this was permission to read the thing and not permission to take anything out of it.*

"Chen." Tanaka was at the archway, and her face was doing something Lyra had not seen it do. "You are not going in. Look at me. You are not going in."

"I'm the only one who can read the—"

"I know you are. That is not the question." Tanaka came about four steps closer. "Miss Chen, in nineteen years I have brought about two thousand students through that arch and I have watched about nine of them be *useful* in it, and there is a reason there have only ever been nine, and it is that the Maze is calibrated to whoever is in it, and it is calibrated by *touch*, and it takes about eleven minutes, and it is not on the schedule, and if you go in there and it calibrates to you in front of a reading of thirty-one per cent then it will be *inside you* in a way that we cannot take out, and I have seen it happen once, in 1997, to a woman of twenty-four, and she is a room on the third floor of the east dormitory with nobody in it."

Lyra stood on the wet flagstones and had the single most useful four seconds of her life.

"Then send somebody who does not calibrate," she said. "Send Mr. Reyes. He is not visual. Send him and send Ms. Washington, because she can feel a structure at nine feet, and send Mr. Park, because he has the diagram and he will not go past the second door, and *I* will read the sheet, from outside, over a radio, and if the number changes I will tell you, and you will pull them out, and you will not need me in there at all."

"That is not how the Maze—"

"Professor Tanaka." Lyra's voice came out steadier than she had any right to expect from a person who had been awake for twenty-nine hours. "Eleven minutes. You said eleven minutes. I have been in this building nine days and I have been told the number on a board is not a percentage of anything, and that a piece of paper in a sketchbook is not a delivery, and that a photograph is a photograph. I have been told that *discovering a thing does not make the thing bigger.* Somebody told me that in a bookshop in August, in front of my father, out loud, and I have thought about it every day."

Tanaka looked at her for about two seconds.

"You would have been a good one," she said. "Send the boy and the girl. Park stays with me and the sheet. And you stand at that archway and you read me the number out loud every two minutes, and if I say *out*, you do not argue with me, and if you argue with me I will put you in a corridor and you will be extremely angry, and I will be right, and we will both know it."

---

They went in at 2:40. The three of them.

Lyra stood at the archway in the rain with a radio in one hand and a sheet of paper in the other and read out the number every two minutes for one hour and eleven minutes, and she has kept the sheet, and it is in a tin in a kitchen on a street called Ellery.

"Twenty-nine."

"Twenty-nine."

"Thirty."

"That's the anteroom board. What have you got on the sheet?"

"Thirty-one. And there's a second number, Professor, and it's in a different pencil, and the second number is a *depth*—"

At 3:52 the number on the sheet went to forty-four, and the archway *moved*.

## INSIDE

Milo Reyes has written four pages about the inside of the Thread Maze and they are in the record and they are the only four pages in the whole of the Inquiry that anybody has ever asked to be withdrawn, and the request was made by him, in 2016, and it was refused, and he asked again, and the reason it was refused is in the Chair's minute, and the Chair wrote it himself, on the same day, twenty-nine years after the afternoon, which was not an accident either:

> *Mr. Reyes asks that the record of his experience be removed. Mr. Reyes is now forty-five and teaches in a conservatorium and is the foremost authority in his field, and he has explained to me that the reason he wants it removed is not shame and is that he has taught the material for twelve years and there is a version of the afternoon in which he is a boy who was frightened, and he does not want that to be the thing that is remembered.*
>
> *I have declined, for the following reasons, which I have put in the minute so that they are on the record and not merely in my head.*
>
> *One. The Inquiry's finding is that the events of September 2013 were not caused by the bravery of four teenagers. It is the central finding and the four of them have been extremely careful never to claim otherwise. The record of the afternoon is the primary evidence for that finding. If it is removed, the finding is a story.*
>
> *Two. Mr. Reyes is not frightened in the record. He is *accurate* in the record, and frightened, and the two are not the same thing, and in twelve years of teaching he has made a career out of the difference, and the record is the best example of it in this building.*
>
> *Three. A building that removes a record because the person in it has become somebody who does not want it is a building that will eventually remove a record because the person in it is not important enough to object. We have spent seventy years doing that to other people. I am not doing it in November. — The Chair, 4 November 2042*

---

The three of them went in at 2:40 and came out at 3:53, which is one hour and thirteen minutes, and Tanaka had said eleven, and Eli Park had said eleven, and Dr. Mei-Hua Chen had said the calibration takes about eleven minutes, and it took an hour and thirteen because the Maze was reconfiguring around them the entire time and a structure under the east dormitory was pulling on it.

Milo Reyes's account, in part, because the finding of the Inquiry rests on it and because Lyra Chen has asked that it be read in full at every conference she has ever spoken at, which she has done four times:

> *We were in the second chamber and Milo could hear the number.*
>
> *He has said, and has said in print, and I am going to say it too because he has asked me to: he could hear it. Not the corridor. The number. He said it was "like a note that goes up and comes down, eleven seconds, and it was in my teeth, and by the fourth time I had counted it and by the fortieth time I had written it in a notebook, and by the eightieth time I understood that it was not a number at all, it was a *breath*."*
>
> *Eli Park got us to the third chamber. There is a great deal written in the record about the third chamber and I want to say one thing about it which is not in the record: it was the most frightening place any of us have ever been and I did not see any of it, and I have spent the rest of my life being told that I saw a great deal, and I want it entered that I saw a great deal of *a wall*, and that I have never seen anything else, and that I was extremely brave about a wall.*
>
> *Zara got us to the seventh chamber. She did not go forward. She went *sideways*, which none of us expected and which she has said was the only decision she made in the next ten months that she has not had to take back, and she stopped in a doorway and put both hands on the stone on either side of it and said: "There is a woman here."*
>
> *And then she said, "No. There is a *hole* where a woman is."*
>
> *And then she said, "And it is *older than the building*."*

---

Lyra Chen was standing at the archway in the rain at 3:53 with a radio in her hand and forty-four per cent on a piece of paper, and the archway moved, and what came out of it was Milo Reyes, on his hands and knees, and Eli Park, and Zara Washington, and the three of them were carrying a boy of fourteen who was not unconscious and was *singing*.

He was singing a hymn. He was singing it in a language nobody in the group recognised and it took the Inquiry three weeks to identify it as something from a revivalist church in Leeds, and James Chen has never been asked about it, and has said in one published interview that he does not remember any of it and has not been able to stop humming it.

## WHAT THE FLOOR WAS

He was in the middle of the seventh chamber, on the floor, with his hands flat on a cracked hearthstone, and the number on his sheet had gone from forty-four to nineteen in about four seconds and had not gone back up.

Eli Park's account of the room, which is four sentences long and which the Chair has said is the four most useful sentences in nine hundred pages:

"Professor Tanaka's arch had a cache on it and the cache was a *rim* and the rim had a *depth*, and the depth was about nine feet, and in the bottom of it was the machine, and the machine was not on the drawing.

"Mr. Reyes found the boy. Ms. Washington found the hole. I found the drawing in a book in 2009 in a different city and I have never been able to work out why the man who wrote that book was not looking for a machine and why nobody in this building has ever read that book, and I have decided that the answer is that he was looking for a *theory* and there was nothing in the world to make him look for a *thing*, and that is the same reason the Academy has had a Registry for six years."

The Inquiry's finding on the seventh chamber, day three, and it is one line:

> *The installation beneath the east dormitory is a working device. It is not a diagram, a model, a prototype, or a plan. It was drawing from the ward network at approximately nineteen per cent of a load of four on the evening of the thirteenth of September 2013, it had been running since at least the second of September 2013 in the form in which it presently exists, and it was in use before the war.*

## THE OTHER THING IN THE ROOM

It was not a machine in a room. It was a machine *under* a machine, and Professor Tanaka's arch was not the top of it.

"There were two of them," said the technician, in 2019, at a hearing nobody had expected to need, in a room in Lyon. "I want to say the thing that nobody said in 2013 and that I have had six years to sit with.

"There was the device, in the chamber, in the east dormitory, that had a fourteen-year-old boy on the floor of it. And there was the *other* one, under the device, in the part that was not on the inventory, in a housing that was not a housing, that had been *grown* — and the word I want is grown, because I have looked at a great deal of engineering and I have never seen anything grown, and I have never seen anything that had been sealed by a man and then sat under a campus for seventy-two years being extremely well-behaved and extremely expensive to maintain.

"And on the thirteenth of September 2013, at about four o'clock in the afternoon, the device on the floor of the seventh chamber and the housing underneath it were *both running*, and they had been running *against each other*, and that is the reason the counter moved and that is the reason the boy could hear it, and that is the reason the archway moved.

"And when the archway came down on the east side at twenty past four on the Friday afternoon, it came down on *both of them*, and it is the only time in the history of this building that a structural collapse has improved a matter, and I have been a technician for twenty-nine years and I have never said a sentence like that one in a room and I would like it in the transcript, and I would like somebody afterwards to explain to me why a seventy-two-year-old accident in a room under a building full of children was the best thing that happened to any of us."

## THE COLLAPSE

They came out at 3:53. The archway came down at 4:20.

Twenty-seven minutes, which is the margin, and the margin is the entire reason there is a register of case forty-one and a fifth board and an Act that has been amended in a fortnight, and the margin is the following:

Tanaka had four wardens and a radio and a nine-year-old's assessment rota and the authority of the most senior instructor on the ground. Dr. Chen had a Registrar's emergency order, which is a piece of paper that closes a building. And Marcus Harlow — who had a warrant from the Council of Traditions authorising a supervised demonstration, who had been in the building for two days without permission, and who had eleven weeks in him — walked into a courtyard at 3:56 and said, in front of four wardens, in front of a registrar, in front of a Headmistress on a line, and in front of two hundred and eleven students who were in a lecture hall on the other side of a wall:

"Take me out. Put me in a corridor with a chair. I have an instrument in that building and I have not read it since Monday and I am going to read it in a corridor with two wardens outside, and if I come out of that corridor before the arch comes down you can arrest me, and if I do not come out of that corridor you can tell the Council that a member of this faculty has finally done one thing correctly."

He was in a corridor with a chair from 4:04 until 9:40 that evening, and four wardens stood outside, and he sent a headset to Tanaka at 4:31, and the headset is in the record, and it is four minutes long, and the Chair has read it out four times, and it is four minutes of a man of fifty-four giving the operating instructions of a device he built, to an instructor, in a language that is almost entirely about *load paths*, and about a fault in the east lattice about a hand's width wide, and the last forty seconds of it are this:

"—and the *rim* is not part of it. The rim is a reading, not a structure. Do not let anybody put a hand near the rim. That is what happened to the boy and it is what happened to me in a demonstration hall and it is what happened to a woman of twenty-four in a garden, and it is a *grazing* incidence, and every one of us has made the mistake, and I have made it eleven times, and I am asking you, as a professional favour between two people who have never liked each other, to let the building come down on the cage and not on the boy, and I am aware that I am asking a woman to destroy eleven years of my work, and I would like it minuted that I asked."

The archway came down on the east side at twenty past four in the afternoon on Friday the thirteenth of September and it took forty-one seconds and it was a controlled collapse from underneath, and there is no record of anybody being hurt, and the Court's finding in 2016 was that the building's own Consultant Engineer had a written plan for it in a drawer from 1974, and that the plan was not actioned, and that the man who wrote it had been asked about it once, in 2010, and had written back a four-line letter which is in the record, and which ends: *and if you are asking me whether the arch can survive a collapse of that magnitude, the answer is that the arch was designed by people who expected it, and I do not know who they were, and I have looked.*

---

Lyra Chen was in a corridor at eleven minutes past four on the thirteenth of September with a radio in her hand and a sheet of paper in her other hand and forty-four per cent on it, and it went to nineteen and stayed at nineteen, and then, at 4:38, the sheet went blank.

"That is the *chart* going," said Tanaka, on the radio. "Lyra. That is his chart going. He is fine. Get the Headmistress. Get her *now.*"

And Lyra Chen, who had been in the building for ten days, and who had lost nothing yet except the ability to look at a piece of paper from a distance, went and found Eleanor Weaver, and told her, in a corridor, in about eleven words, that a boy called James Chen was on the floor of the seventh chamber of a building that had just come down and was alive, and that he was in a part of it that two hundred people in this Academy did not know about, and that a professor had been in a corridor with a chair and had come out of it at 9:40, and that she needed to know all of it, in a corridor, in daylight, at twenty past five on a Saturday afternoon, with a witness, in writing.

Which is exactly what Eli Park had said in a cellar, on the Friday, about a piece of paper in a sketchbook, and had been laughed at for.

And which is the last thing that happened in Book One that is a *decision* rather than a *reaction*, and it is the reason a woman who had been Headmistress for six years spent the next nine days doing paperwork on purpose, with the door in the room, and is the reason a Registrar of sixty-eight was sixty-eight seconds ahead of everybody for eleven days, and is the reason there is a fifth board.
