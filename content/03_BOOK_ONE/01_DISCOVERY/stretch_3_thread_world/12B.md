## WHY THIS IS HERE

The note on the outside of the file is in a registrar's hand and is four lines long, and it is the only reason this document is in a book at all.

> *This is a document written on the eleventh of September 2013 at about two hours in the morning, by the member of faculty whose name is on it, in his own hand, on the back of a form. It was not sent to anybody. It was found on the fourteenth, in his desk, by the man who has been cleaning out that office for four years, and he read about four lines of it and then he put it in a file and went home at six o'clock on the fourteenth, which is two hours before his shift ended, and he has given a statement about that and the statement is two lines long and it says: **"I am not the person who should be the one to have read that."***
>
> *The first week of term, Dr. Chen was absent from a seminar, and the events of the eighteenth are in the record. Nothing in this file was known to the subject of the Inquiry, or to the patient, or to any of the four young people, until the nineteenth of October.*
>
> *I have put it in the book because the alternative is that the person who took a girl of fifteen out of this building on the eleventh of September remains a man whose actions began on the fourteenth, and that is false, and it was false in 2002 as well. — M-H. Chen, Registrar*

## THE ENTRY

*I am writing this at two o'clock in the morning on the eleventh of September and I am writing it because I have decided, on the stairs, four minutes ago, that I am going to do something tomorrow that I will not be able to describe accurately afterwards, and that the description is always the only part anybody ever checks.*

*The girl is in a room four floors up. She is asleep. She has been asleep since half past nine. She is fifteen. She has been in this building for two days, and she is here because her sister put her on a coach, and her sister is a third-year who has been extremely careful about something for four years and did not want to be asked about it in a corridor, and I know the something, and I have known it since April, and I have not asked her about it either, and I have decided — on the stairs, four minutes ago, at about ten to two in the morning — that I am going to go and ask Elara Vance, and that I am going to ask her in a way that does not make her feel stupid.*

Here is the arithmetic, and I am going to put it down because I have been told, by a woman in a demonstration hall in August whom I did not manage to help, that putting it down is the whole of the difference between a person and a machine.

The machine under this mountain needs a resonance to hold an open circuit, and has needed one since 1997, and I have spent eleven years looking for a body that will take it. In 2002 I found a document in a drawer in this building that says the Animus Argenti is a bridge, and I have spent eleven years trying to build a machine out of a sentence.

Elara Vance is a bridge.

That is not a metaphor and I hate it. It is a measurement. She came in on the ninth with a signature like a lit window, and on the tenth I put her in a calibration chair and read her for four hours, and I have had that reading eleven times before, in children, and every one of the eleven had a floor to stand on, and Elara Vance has no floor. She is the only signature I have ever seen that *already is* the thing the machine wants.

I have written that sentence in four files since April and I have not once written the next sentence, and the next sentence is the one that says what the machine does with a signature that is already the thing it wants, and the answer is: *it holds the circuit*, and the way it holds it is by taking the person and keeping the part of them that is holding.

That is what happened in a garden in April 1997, and it took nine seconds, and I stood in it.

And here is the arithmetic, and it is correct, and I want it in the record in my own hand because I know what is going to happen to this document:

*She will live. The vessel is intact. She will not bleed and she will not scar and she will be, in a physical sense, entirely well. She will lose the ability to perceive threads and she will lose the ability to be perceived by anybody in her family, and she will be fifteen, and she will be a bridge for the rest of her life, and I do not know how to say any of that to a person in a chair.*

*Fourteen days. In fourteen days the Southern Nexus fails and eleven thousand people find out.*

*And she is the only one in the world.*

---

I went up at ten past two and I sat on the end of the bed in a room with a window and a girl in it and I did the only thing I am good at, which is ask a question.

"Miss Vance. I am going to ask you three things and you are allowed to say no to all of them and I am going to take a no as final, and I want that on the card, and I am going to hand the card to you and not to your sister, because you are fifteen and your sister is eighteen and the difference between those two numbers is the difference between a person and a signature."

She said, "What's it for?"

And I said — and I have this in the voice memo I took at the top of the stairs at ten to two, which I have now also destroyed, because I understood about four seconds after pressing the button what I had done, and I want that on the record too, and it is the worst thing in this file:

"Miss Vance, I am going to offer you a place on a specialist programme. It is a four-week residential course in applied thread dynamics. It is the most advanced programme in this field. I am going to be honest with you about three things, because I have discovered in eleven years that the young people I have harmed most were the ones I was honest with, and the ones I damaged second were the ones I lied to, and I have never worked out why that is true and I have stopped trying.

"It is residential and it is supervised and you will be taught by four people, one of whom is me.

"It is not a gift, and nobody has proved that there is any such thing, and I have spent eleven years pretending otherwise in public because a committee requires a category.

"And it will change you, and I do not know how, and I am telling you that I do not know, and I have read every paper there is and I do not know, and *I am telling you that* because a man put those exact words in a lecture hall three days ago in front of two hundred people and I have thought about nothing else since, and it is the only decent thing anybody has done in this building in eleven years and he did it by accident while trying to recruit her."

She asked, "What kind of change?"

And I said, "The kind that does not come back."

And I sat on the end of a bed in a dormitory at twenty to three in the morning and let a fifteen-year-old think about it, which is the correct procedure, and which I had not used eleven times in eleven years, because eleven times in eleven years I had a form in front of me with a number on it, and a number is a *terrible* thing to have in front of you at twenty to three in the morning, and I knew it, and I did it anyway, and I want that on the record in the order in which it happened.

She said, "Can I ring my mother?"

I said yes.

She rang her mother. She spoke for four minutes, and Lyra, four floors up and nine days into a term in which she had already been the reason two men in a circle could not finish an argument, slept through all of it.

She spoke for four minutes and then she said, "She says to ask whether there are other people like me," and I said, "There are four. In the world. That is not an exaggeration and I would ask you to please not repeat it as one, because in 2002 a woman of twenty-four was told that in a letter and repeated it in a lecture hall and about forty people wrote down the number."

And Elara Vance said, "Then I'd like to do it," and signed the card, in her own hand, with a pen, and it says:

> **VOLUNTEER**

and the date, and the time, and the name of the person who offered it, and I did not read it, and I have written that sentence in this file four times now and I am going to stop.

## THE ENTRY, LATER

*Eighteen September, 11:40 p.m. Sub-level 3.*

*Seven days. I have written four thousand words and I have not written the part.*

Here is what I did not write on the eleventh.

I did not write that on the eleventh — on the eleventh — she asked me, back in the calibration chair, with a chart on the wall behind her, whether anyone had asked *her* what the machine was going to be.

I said, "Not yet."

And she said, "Why not?"

And I said, "Because the answer is complicated and you are fifteen and I did not want to give you a complicated answer at two o'clock on a Wednesday."

And then I said the other thing. I have been trying for seven days to work out why I said it, and the best I have is that it was in the room and it was the wrong thing, and I have never in my life been in a room where the wrong thing was simply *available*, and in the room, and nobody in the corridor to see me take it.

I said: "You will be the first, Miss Vance. In the programme. I have not told anybody that, because there are four of you and there is one place, and the place goes to whoever is ready, and I have spent seven days deciding that you were ready, and I have not asked you, and I would like to hear you say it now."

And Elara Vance — fifteen years old, four days in a building, with a card on the table that said *volunteer* and a sister asleep on a floor two above her and a mother on a telephone in a town four hundred miles away who had been told by a *letter* that her daughter was in a specialist programme for gifted young people and who had written back in February to ask what the curriculum was and had had a form posted to her, and who had, in April, put her daughter on a coach —

Elara Vance said, "You decided."

And I said, "Yes."

And she said, "I want to know what happens to me."

And I told her. All of it. In order. For about eleven minutes, in a calibration room, on a Wednesday afternoon in a building in a valley, to a child.

And then I asked her, and she signed, and I did not read the card.

And here is the part I have not been able to write for seven days, and it is two sentences long, and I am going to write it and then I am going to close this file:

*She asked me what happens to her and I told her the truth.*

*She did not understand the truth, and I knew she had not understood it, and I watched her not understand it, and I let her sign.*

---

*End of entry. No further entry. The file is closed because there is nothing after the seventh day. The Council's question is always the same and it is always about the eleventh, and this document is the answer to it, and it is four thousand words long, and the answer is in the second sentence of the fourth page, and it is that a man of fifty-four told a child the truth in eleven minutes and she signed a card, and he did not read the card, and he watched her not understand him.*

*And I would like it recorded — by whoever reads this in a book, which I do not expect and have not prepared for — that in the four days before the second of October a girl of sixteen asked thirty-nine people in a cage what they wanted, and that on the second of October she had been standing in a fault for four days holding it open, and that somebody with a stick and a model in a case lined it without going into it, and that thirty-seven of them said out loud that they wanted to be let go, and that she honoured it, and that thirty-seven of them are dead.*

*And I would like it recorded that I was in that building on the second of October and that I was the reason there was something to line.*

*And I would like it recorded, in my own hand, in this file, with a date, that this is the fifth option and I did not propose it and I have spent eleven years arguing that it was impossible, and that I was wrong, and that the person who was right was a sixteen-year-old with a napkin and a woman with a tube of silk and a man who was going to be dead in a week.*

*There is no forgiveness in this. I want to be extremely clear, because I have read four hundred pages of this Inquiry and I know exactly what everybody in it is hoping for, and I have been the man who leaves a door open that he is going to walk through, and this time I am not going to do it.*

*Her name was Elara Vance. Her mother's name is Ines. Her sister's name is Elena and her sister is twenty-nine feet above her and she has been sleeping on the other side of a wall for four days and she does not know, and her sister has spent four years building a thing for her, and it is in a garage, and I have never seen it, and I am going to die not having seen it, and that is correct, and it is the only thing in this file that I have got right.*
