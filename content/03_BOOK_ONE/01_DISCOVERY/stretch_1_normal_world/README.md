## Purpose and question

Lyra's gift belongs to a household before it belongs to a plot, and the
household has to be argued about. **Question:** what does it cost to be the
person in this family who can see?

**Entry:** a girl who has been drawing connections for six years. **Exit:** she
has decided to go, her grandmother has told her what her mother was, her father
has stopped pretending not to know, and three people are in a kitchen having
disagreed about it.

## What changed

- The acceptance beat is no longer repeated. Ch. 3 decides; Ch. 4 informs and
  quarrels.
- The pendant in Ch. 2 is an **oval absence in the dust**, paid off in Ch. 4
  when Nai Nai produces it. Previously it was in the studio in Ch. 2 and in
  Nai Nai's kitchen in Ch. 4.
- The Academy letter gives the venue, the time and the address, and the frame in
  Ch. 1 happens there. `Moonrise Books` and `Iris Bookstore & Cafe` are one
  place.
- Nai Nai's reason for waiting five years is given, and the wait is now eleven.
- The thread tradition is anchored to a **Sino-Vietnamese family from Chợ Lớn**,
  with `Bàba` and `Nai Nai` in the household's own language, rather than being
  decorative detail.
- Wei's "Alexander" is marked as a given name used by one person, so that the
  authority figure of Ch. 9 and the father of Ch. 25 cannot be confused.

## Exit test

A reader can say who in that house is a seer, who has been keeping what, for
how long, and what the argument in the kitchen was actually about.
