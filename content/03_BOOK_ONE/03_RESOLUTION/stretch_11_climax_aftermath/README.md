## What changed

- **The students do not decide what happens to Harlow**, and Eli rules out the
  attractive version of that decision *before* it can happen: the charges are not
  theirs to drop, and nineteen families have no representative in the building.
- **Remorse is explicitly not evidence.** Zara states on the record that it is a
  state and not a measurement, and that a policy built on her perceptions would
  be a catastrophe.
- **Milo asks a question that can be checked**, and gets a fact instead of a
  feeling: a hundred and forty days of a log and a question about the twelfth
  minute, which is still unanswered, picked up by exactly one person — a doctoral
  student in Vienna, twenty-six, who has never met him (`36:101`).
- **The cost is permanent**: five documents leave in five hands, nineteen
  descendants are traced and told by someone who is not him, and his research
  access is gone outright — not suspended, including the years he would have
  had.
- **Wei Chen is not in Ch. 36.** The library scene with a living father is gone.
  What is left is an estate office with a clipboard, a grandmother saying *not
  yet, con, not yet*, and thirty-one tea rings in a kitchen.
- **The bounded contact has stopped coming**, and the text says what Lyra does
  with that: she treats it as a Tuesday.
- The four futures are stated as **plans with limits**, not as retirements. Milo
  is not a prodigy; Lyra says out loud that she is **sixteen** and does not know
  what she is doing. *Corrected 2026-09-28:* this line said "seventeen", and
  attributed it to something she says aloud. **She never says seventeen** — the
  word belongs to Ben Ruiz, Maya Ortiz and Thandiwe Mbeki, who are the depleted
  students on the board. Lyra is sixteen throughout, per
  `02_CHARACTERS/protagonist_lyra.md` and `CANON.md` §1.3.
