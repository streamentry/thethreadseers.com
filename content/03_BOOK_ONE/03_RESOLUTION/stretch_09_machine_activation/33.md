## THE FOUR HOURS

"One. The bleed has not restarted and is not going to, because the source of the bleed was a feedback loop between a seer and a man, and the seer is standing in a fault and the man is lying in a housing, and the loop does not close and cannot close and will not close in either of us, ever again."

The room was very quiet, and somewhere up the corridor somebody was doing something with a kettle, and it was the most ordinary sound Lyra Chen had ever heard.

"Did Mum come?" she said.

Her father's face did something she had never seen it do.

"No," he said. "That is the thing I have been getting wrong since Sunday night, and I have had a spinal block and eight hours and I have got it exactly wrong, and I am going to tell you, and then I am going to sleep.

Lyra put her hand on the side of the trolley.

"It is not remotely a comforting thing," her father agreed, "and I have been trying to find the version that is, for eight hours, and there is not one, and I have concluded that this is the correct outcome, and that the reason everybody in this family has been in such a mess for two generations is that we have all been waiting for a door."

What she did instead, in the two days after her father stopped breathing properly, was sit in two rooms and did not do anything, and was extremely good at it, which everybody in the building had discovered she could be, and which was the single most frightening thing about her for the rest of the year.

## WHAT WAS DONE
