what do the people he harmed need, independently of his remorse?

## What changed

- **The apparition is not authenticated and is not claimed to be Mei.** Lyra
  says the only evidence in the room is wanting; Harlow declines to speak to it,
  because it is the only participant in the conversation that was never asked.
- **Lyra does not forgive him.** Not never — because none of the people his
  decision was about are in the room, and she is sixteen and has spent a month
  learning the difference between deciding and being allowed to.
- **`No longer dangerous` is gone.** What has happened is that he has stopped.
  That is a different thing and it is not a stage.
- **Ch. 34A is the epilogue's Concord, moved here** so that the one-week repair
  is read before the six-week farewell.
- The Concord now has an **enabling sequence and a disagreement**: Adebayo
  Adeyemi and the Keeper of the Quipus argue about whether an answer exists at
  all, and the turn is that Lyra's hands find a *list of purposes* — forty-one
  sentences, ending in *Held: To be held* — which reframes the machine as an
  archive that was wired to a power supply.
- The Andean khipu/quipu misattribution is corrected in an author's aside, not
  repeated as tradition. **Other cultural items in this chapter are not
  authenticated and need a reader**; see `VERIFICATION.md`.

## Exit test

The reader understands that remorse may be real and entirely insufficient, that
grief has not been subordinated to the man who caused it, and that the repair
is work done by people who do not agree with one another.
